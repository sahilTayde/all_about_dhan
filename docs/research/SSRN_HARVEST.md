# SSRN / arXiv / NSE harvest for NIFTY option buys

Paper research only. Nothing here is a live order, a win-rate claim, or a strategy to ship. Every paper below was opened in this pass (PDF, working-paper reprint, or the abstract page when the PDF was blocked). Numbers are copied from those pages. If a figure was not on the pages opened, it is not filled in.

This pass records **51 new papers** plus the already-tested set. **32** of the new papers state a rule or a direct statistical test a programmer can rerun (entry, a calendar dummy, or a regression with the variables named). The other 19 were opened and are marked in the JSON as not a buy rule, because the pages did not fix an entry, a side, and an exit. Padding the list with papers that were not opened would have been worse than stopping here.

**Our tape, used to mark data fit:** 1-minute index OHLC for 5 years; 60-second option-chain snapshots with LTP, volume and OI for 13–15 strikes over 3 years; India VIX daily; bid/ask depth only from Monday. Signed trades, historical depth, news text, a stock cross-section, FII/DII prints, and true dealer inventory are not in that tape.

Machine-readable copy of every record: `docs/research/ssrn_harvest.json`.

## Top 15 to test next

Ranked for (a) whether our tape can build the signal, (b) whether a long call or put with a same-day exit could use it (we do not want a long ATM hold), and (c) how hard the evidence is. A high rank can mean “test this and expect it to fail.” That still saves a bad option buy.

**1. Mesfin, structural limits of OHLCV intraday signals (2026).** Run his gates on Nifty before buying any opening-range or gap breakout. On Micro E-mini Nasdaq futures, 947 days of 5-minute bars, 2021–2025, walk-forward, a pass needed T ≥ 2 on out-of-sample net returns, at least 30 trades per fold, a positive net after a 2-point round trip, and the same sign across years. Eleven of fourteen families never clear the 2-point friction (gross edge 0.07 to 1.50 points). Opening-range breakout long, 15-bar hold: gross +4.82 points, net +2.82, T = 0.88, 447 out-of-sample trades, fails (Table 13). An 80.7% stop-out rate at a 20-point stop means the breakout is mostly a reversal, not a continuation. Our 1-minute index can rebuild 5-minute bars. This is US futures, not Nifty, and the friction number is MNQ points, not rupees.

**2. Mesfin, volatility-volume-gap day tag (2026).** Code the tag. Do not trade it yet. A day is tagged only when three things are all above their expanding-window 66.7th percentile up to the prior day: the first-30-minute return, the absolute overnight gap, and first-bar volume versus its 20-day rolling baseline. It fires on 40 of 947 days (4.2% in Table 2; the abstract says roughly 4.4%). The next session’s mean return is +0.295% on tagged days versus +0.039% otherwise, a spread of +0.256% or 25.6 basis points (Table 3). 77.6% of tagged paths (97 of 125) give back the intraday peak by the close, mean giveback 11.73 points (Table 5). The fade he actually trades — regression of the first 30 minutes on the last 30 minutes — has 127 out-of-sample trades, mean net +7.80 points, T = +1.46, win rate 52.0%, and 2024 is negative (−26.75 points). He marks it FAIL. The close fade at 15:30 never gets 30 trades. Useful as a day label on our OHLC and volume. Not an option entry.

**3. Bollerslev, Tauchen and Zhou, variance risk premium (2009).** Use this as a “do not buy expensive options” filter, not as a hold. Variance risk premium is model-free implied variance minus realized variance from high-frequency returns. On monthly S&P data, January 1990–December 2007, the quarterly-return regression on that premium has t = 2.86 and adjusted R² = 6.82% (the monthly-horizon table in the paper). High premium predicts high future equity returns, which is the signature of rich implied vol, not of cheap options. They also cite Beckers and Bouten (2005): a timing rule based on the ratio of implied to historical volatility doubles the Sharpe ratio of a constant S&P 500 exposure. We can build a cousin with daily India VIX and 1-minute realized variance. The horizon in their table is one to several months, so it must not become a multi-day ATM long. US equities, not India. Costs are not a trading P&L in this paper.

**4. Fleming, Kirby and Ostdiek, volatility timing (2001).** Size the option budget down when recent realized variance is high. This is the parent of Moreira–Muir, which is already tested and passed, so do not reopen Moreira–Muir as a new idea. Fleming, Kirby and Ostdiek study daily stock, bond and gold returns. In their simulations, when volatility persistence matches a GARCH, the estimated fee a risk-averse investor would pay to switch from the static portfolio exceeds 170 basis points a year; one reported mean is 173 basis points, and another specification is 241. A one-way futures cost of about 3.28% annualized (their S&P futures example) does not remove the edge; they say costs would need to be at least six times that. Our 1-minute index can make the variance input. This is a size rule for a signal we already have, not a new entry, and it is not an India option sample.

**5. Holmberg, Lönnbark and Lundström, opening-range breakout (2013), read inside the Lundström thesis reprint.** Buy the break only when realized vol is in a high state, and flat by the close. The rule: if price trades through a band set from a normal-return range around the open, go long above it or short below it, and exit at the cash close. On US crude oil futures, 30 March 1983–26 January 2011, the full sample beats zero, but split samples are significantly positive only in 12 October 2001–26 January 2011, which includes the crisis. The thesis summary of the follow-up (Paper II) says the average daily ORB return differs by around 200 basis points between the highest and lowest volatility state on crude oil, and by around 150 basis points on S&P 500 futures. The reprint assumes a zero spread and zero commission when it describes the threshold. Mesfin (item 1) later fails an unconditional ORB after costs. Test only the high-vol slice, on Nifty, same-day option exit. US futures, not India.

**6. K. Kiran Kumar, weekly Nifty options and expiry (2022).** Do not buy a monthly-expiry drift. Daily Nifty closes, 11 February 2018–10 February 2020, split at the February 2019 start of weekly index options. Before weeklies, the paper finds an upward price shift around expiry and a transitory rise in volatility. After weeklies, those effects disappear in a GARCH with an expiry dummy and a post-introduction interaction. No coefficient is copied here because the result tables were not in the extract used; the claim above is the abstract and the model section. Sample ends February 2020, and Nifty’s expiry weekday has since moved, so the Thursday dummy is stale. Our daily index, built from 1-minute bars, is enough to re-estimate the dummy. This is a calendar fact, not a call/put entry.

**7. Ni, Pearson and Poteshman, strike clustering on option expiry (2005).** On expiry, test whether Nifty settles nearer the strike with the most open interest than it does on other days. If it does, the option buy is a same-day drift toward that strike, closed at the settlement, not an overnight ATM hold. On US optionable stocks they estimate that returns are altered by an average of at least 16.5 basis points per expiry (body calculation 16.57 bps), at least about 2% of names are affected, and the market-cap shift is at least $9.1 billion per expiry date. Hedge rebalancing and proprietary-trader writing both show up in their logits. We have strikes, LTP and OI, so a pin distance is computable on the 13–15 strikes we store. We do not have dealer inventory. US single stocks, not a Nifty index. See item 8 before believing the pin.

**8. Gupta, Metia and Trivedi, NSE option expiry (2003).** Treat the US pin result as unproven in India until our own tape says otherwise. They study Reliance, Satyam, Infosys, Telco and HDFC, tick data, June 2000–August 2002 (volume tables extend the comparison into 2003). Expiry volume is higher than the prior Thursday and the next day on average, but mean differences are not statistically significant on 2001–2002 data. Satyam’s expiry volume differs from the day before and the day after in 2002–2003, and still not from the control Thursday. Returns are not significantly different. HDFC closed within 3 rupees of a strike 18.52% of the time, against a no-pin benchmark they put near 20.8%, so they do not find pinning. Old, five stocks, not the index, and the market is not today’s weekly complex. Still the only India pinning test opened in this pass.

**9. Chakrabarti and K. Kiran Kumar, Nifty option order flow and the variance premium (2016 conference paper).** Do not code a live rule. The idea is testable later with a proxy. Volatility demand is the vega-weighted order imbalance of calls and puts at the same strike and expiry, averaged across five NSE snapshots (11:00, 12:00, 13:00, 14:00, 15:00). They regress the daily change in the variance premium (India VIX squared, scaled to a month, minus realized variance from 5-minute Nifty returns) on that demand by moneyness. The most expensive options’ demand is what moves the premium. A positive change in the premium lines up with a positive innovation in realized variance. Sample: 1 July 2015–31 December 2015 only. In that window, calls are 51.59% of option trades and puts 48.41%; ATM calls 34.36%, ATM puts 30.03% (trading-activity section). We do not have signed trades or a historical book, so the imbalance cannot be rebuilt. Six months, in-sample regression, no option-buy P&L, no costs.

**10. Kakushadze, four-factor overnight model and an intraday residual fade (2015).** Only worth a test if it beats the plain overnight-gap fade we already ran. Factors, all from the prior day: log price, prior open-to-close return, an intraday high-low volatility, and a volume liquidity factor. The check trade buys the residual at the open and sells at the close, top-2000 US stocks by dollar volume, five years ending 5 September 2014, no costs and no slippage. He says the point is to see whether the factors add anything, not to claim a strategy. Fama–MacBeth t-statistics are in his Tables 1–3; the P&L paths are Figures 1–2 and Table 5, and this pass did not copy a single P&L number off those figures. We do not have a stock cross-section. An index-only version is a different, weaker test. US, no costs.

**11. Lin, Lin and Zhang, volume-price MACD (2026).** Low priority, and not an intraday option buy. Daily SPY, QQQ and DIA, train 2018–2022 (1,259 days), test 2023 through February 2026 (791 days). A sensitivity λ is chosen on the train set. For SPY the chosen values are λ = 0.90 (MACD plus λ) and λ = 0.88 (volume-price MACD), both picked for Sharpe (Table 1). Table 2, SPY test: baseline MACD 40 trades, win rate 50.00%, total P&L $13,612.34, P&L ratio 1.39, Sharpe 0.47, max drawdown −13.78%, expectancy $19.39. MACD with λ: 36 trades, 55.56%, $52,688.94, 2.98, Sharpe 1.35, −8.17%, $121.04. Volume-price MACD: 6 trades, 50.00%, $27,543.78, P&L ratio 8.61, Sharpe 0.96, −6.33%, $365.72. Six test trades cannot support an option overlay, and the hold is multi-day, so theta works against a long premium. US cash index. Our 1-minute bars can rebuild daily OHLC. Costs are not stated as futures commissions; P&L is index-point dollars.

**12. Christensen, Turner and Godsill, hidden Markov intraday momentum (2020).** Do not promote. One year of E-mini S&P 500, 1-minute bars, 2011. A latent momentum state is inferred with a tied Gaussian HMM; the selected state count on their bridge-sampling plot is K = 3, and the stay probability β is set to 0.5. They go with the sign of the latent trend. Pre-cost annualized Sharpe ratios sit around 2.0 (their Figure for long-only versus seasonality versus volatility-ratio). The abstract says “pre-cost the models have a Sharpe ratio in excess of 2.0.” No cost haircut is in that claim. One year, US futures, in-sample risk of a flexible state model. Our 1-minute index can host a replication; expect the Sharpe to shrink once costs and a second sample are added.

**13. Singha, order-flow entropy and the size of moves (2025).** This is a volatility buy, not a CE/PE direction, and we cannot build the input. Entropy comes from a 15-state Markov matrix on signed SPY trades in a 120-second window. Below the 5th percentile of entropy, the next 5-minute absolute return is 15.3 bps, 2.89 times the unconditional mean, t = 12.41. By quintile, low entropy averages 8.14 bps absolute versus 3.75 bps in the highest quintile (Figure 1). Directional accuracy of the associated rule is 45.0% (108 of 240 out-of-sample trades), z = −1.55, p = 0.12. Walk-forward cumulative out-of-sample P&L is +1,126 bps across those 240 trades (Figure 3; the fold table prints 1,125.6). Random entry with the same payoff averages 137 ± 201 bps (z = 4.9 versus the observed 1,126). Sample: 38.5 million SPY trades, 36 days. We do not have signed tick trades. A straddle needs a fast exit; the paper’s edge is the size of the move, which dies if we pay a wide spread. Tiny sample, US, and the payoff contract is only partly specified on the pages read.

**14. Glasserman, Krstovski, Laliberte and Mamaysky, overnight versus intraday (2025).** Do not retest the plain gap fade; that rule is already tested. What this paper adds is a measurement and a news model. Since 2000, the gap between cumulative overnight and cumulative intraday S&P 500 stock returns averages 2.75 basis points per day, about 7.2% per year (page 2, next to Figure 1 and Table 1). Their forecast of which names do well overnight uses news text. We do not have that text. The 2.75 bps figure is a stock-cross-section fact, not a Nifty option P&L, and it is not after an option spread.

**15. Wysocki, learning-to-rank 0DTE short puts (2026).** Read this as the other side of our book. Each day a LightGBM ranker scores eight delta-targeted SPX weekly short puts plus a SKIP, trained on a path Sortino at 1-minute bars, with a margin cap and an abstention rule when the top-two score gap is small. Seven sizing methods produce 2025 out-of-time annualized Sharpes between 4.31 and 5.76. The headline method’s probabilistic Sharpe is 0.964 and the sample max drawdown is −2.28%, against a walk-forward Sharpe range of 1.90 to 3.11. Those Sharpes are on one hold-out year. The economic content that matches the rest of this list: the published edge is short premium. A long option that does not move the same day is fighting that premium. We do not have the full SPX weekly surface or their delta labels. US, option selling, single-year out-of-time, Sharpe too large to trust as a target.

## Already tested — not new ideas

These stay out of the ranked list. They are recorded so the next pass does not rediscover them.

- **Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, market intraday momentum (JFE 2018).** Opened the journal PDF. SPY, 1 February 1993–31 December 2013. First half-hour return, measured from the prior close, predicts the last half-hour. Table 1: slope 6.94 (scaled by 100), R² 1.6%. With the twelfth half-hour as well, joint R² 2.6%. Out-of-sample R² 1.4% (first half-hour alone) and 2.0% (both). A mean-variance investor with risk aversion five gets certainty-equivalent gains of 6.02% a year from the first half-hour, 6.18% with both predictors. Sign timing: average return 6.67% a year, standard deviation 6.19%, Sharpe 1.08, versus buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.29 (average return 6.04%, standard deviation 20.57%). They say the edge survives reasonable costs. US ETF, last-half-hour hold.
- **Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, hedging demand and market intraday momentum (JFE 2021).** Abstract only (SSRN page text; direct PDF blocked). More than 60 futures, equities, bonds, commodities and FX, 1974–2020. Last 30 minutes are positively predicted by the return from the prior close until that window. They tie it to gamma hedging. No table number was on the abstract. Do not re-list.
- **Li, Sakkas and Urquhart, intraday time-series momentum, 16 developed markets (Journal of Financial Markets 2022).** Abstract only. Economically sizable in and out of sample in most countries; stronger when liquidity is low, volatility is high, and information is discrete. No Sharpe on the abstract. Do not re-list.
- **Zarattini, Aziz and Barbon, noise-area intraday momentum on SPY (2024).** Opened the working-paper PDF. Noise area = average same-minute move over the prior 14 days, widened for the overnight gap. Trade only at HH:00 and HH:30, long above the upper band, short below the lower, flat at the close. Abstract, for the version with trailing stops: total return 1,985% net of costs, annualized 19.6%, Sharpe 1.33, 2007 to early 2024. Body, base model without that stop refinement (Table 1 / Figure 3): total return 178%, average annual return 6.2%, volatility 10.9%, Sharpe 0.61, worst day −10.33%. Those two numbers are different strategies. Do not quote 1,985% as the base rule. US SPY. Already tested.
- **Moreira and Muir, volatility-managed portfolios (Journal of Finance 2017; NBER w22208 opened).** This one passed. Scale exposure by the inverse of recent realized variance. For the market, alpha 4.9%, appraisal ratio 0.33, and a 25% increase in the buy-and-hold Sharpe ratio (NBER working paper, June 2016 revision, text around Figure 1). Monthly factor timing, not an intraday option entry. Do not re-list.
- **Liu and Stentoft, gradient boosting on intraday returns (SSRN 4496917, 2023).** Abstract only. “Intraday Stock Predictability Everywhere.” Ensemble and nonlinear models; they write that machine-learning intraday long-short portfolios attain Sharpe ratios of 4 after transaction costs. US stocks, constituent-return features we do not have. Treated as the already-tested GBM paper. The companion Huddleston, Liu and Stentoft market-level paper (SSRN 3726765; abstract Sharpe 0.98 after costs) is the same line of work and is not listed as new.
- **Overnight gap fade.** Already tested by the project. This pass did not reopen one canonical fade paper as a new rule. Glasserman et al. (item 14) measure the overnight-minus-intraday gap; that measurement is not a second fade backtest.

## How a programmer should use this

1. Start with items 1 and 2 on Nifty 1-minute bars. If the opening-range breakout and the gap continuation fail Mesfin’s gates after a realistic option spread, do not buy them.
2. Add item 3 as a filter: skip new long premium when India VIX variance sits far above recent realized variance from the 1-minute index. Item 4 only changes size.
3. Item 5 is the only breakout still worth a same-day call/put test, and only on high realized-vol days, flat by the close.
4. Items 6–9 are expiry and positioning. Re-estimate the expiry dummy on our dates. Measure distance to the max-OI strike. Do not build vega imbalance until signed flow exists.
5. Do not add a new overnight fade (item 14 and the already-tested list). Do not add another volatility-managed monthly book (Moreira–Muir already passed).

## Handoff

### Accepted
- Only papers whose pages were opened in this pass.
- Negative and failed tests (Mesfin, Gupta, the LSTM accuracy paper) count as evidence.
- Moreira–Muir stays a passed sizing result, not a new alpha.

### Rejected
- Invented authors, URLs, or P&Ls.
